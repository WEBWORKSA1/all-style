Seasonal Colour Analysis Explained: The 12 Seasons

What seasonal colour analysis is, how undertone, value and chroma decide your season, and how to test yourself at home.

By the All-Style editors · 8 min read · Updated November 26, 2026

  2. The three dimensions
  3. The 12 seasons at a glance

What colour analysis does

Colour analysis groups people by the colours that harmonise with their natural colouring (skin, hair and eyes). Wearing harmonious colours near the face tends to make skin look clearer and eyes brighter; clashing colours can emphasise shadows and redness. The system is a guide, not a rulebook: you can wear anything, but knowing your best colours makes shopping faster.

The three dimensions

  • Undertone (hue): warm (golden, peachy) or cool (pink, blue). Some people are neutral.
  • Value: how light or dark your overall colouring is.
  • Chroma: how clear and saturated (bright) versus soft and muted your colouring looks.

The four classic seasons combine these: Spring is warm and clear, Summer is cool and soft, Autumn is warm and muted, Winter is cool and clear. The 12-season system splits each into three sub-seasons based on which dimension dominates.

The 12 seasons at a glance

  • Light Spring, Warm Spring, Bright Spring: fresh, warm, clear colours like peach, coral, warm aqua.
  • Light Summer, Cool Summer, Soft Summer: powdery, cool, gentle colours like rose, lavender, slate blue.
  • Soft Autumn, Warm Autumn, Deep Autumn: earthy, muted, rich colours like olive, rust, camel.
  • Deep Winter, Cool Winter, Bright Winter: high-contrast, icy or jewel colours like black, emerald, fuchsia.

How to test yourself at home

  1. Use natural daylight, no makeup, hair pulled back.
  2. Hold a pure white and an off-white (cream) fabric under your face. If cream flatters more, you probably lean warm.
  3. Compare gold and silver jewellery the same way.
  4. Look at the veins on your inner wrist: greenish suggests warm, bluish suggests cool (an imperfect but useful clue).
  5. Judge contrast: is there a big difference between your hair, skin and eyes (high contrast) or are they close (low contrast)?

Using your season

Use your season's colours for tops, scarves and makeup near the face. Neutrals matter most: warm seasons look best in camel, cream and olive; cool seasons in grey, navy and pure white. Shoes and trousers can stray further from your palette. For an expert assessment with physical drapes, consider a professional colour analysis.

FAQ

Is colour analysis scientific?

It's based on colour theory and how colours interact visually, but results involve judgement. Treat it as a practical guide rather than a strict rule.

Can my season change?

Your natural colouring is fairly stable, but greying hair, tanning or dyeing can shift which colours flatter you most.

What if I'm between two seasons?

That's common. Use the colours the two seasons share, which usually sit on your dominant dimension (warmth, depth or softness).
