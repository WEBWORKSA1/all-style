Interior Design Styles People Confuse (and How to Tell Them Apart)

Modern vs contemporary, farmhouse vs rustic, Japandi vs Scandinavian, boho vs maximalist: the clear differences, side by side.

Why the names matter

When you brief a designer, shop online or search for inspiration, the wrong style name sends you to the wrong results. These are the pairs that get mixed up most, with a one-line test for each.

Modern vs contemporary

Modern refers to a specific historical movement (roughly the early-to-mid 20th century): clean lines, function first, little ornament. Contemporary simply means what's current, so it changes: today that's curves, bouclé and sculptural lighting. Test: if it could have been designed in 1955, it's modern; if it looks like this year's design fair, it's contemporary.

Modern farmhouse vs rustic

Modern Farmhouse is bright and crisp: white walls, black metal, a few reclaimed-wood accents. Rustic is darker and rougher: heavy beams, stone, leather. Test: mostly white with wood accents is farmhouse; mostly wood with stone is rustic.

Japandi vs Scandinavian

Both are calm and natural. Scandinavian is lighter, brighter and cosier, with pale woods and soft textiles. Japandi is lower, moodier and more sparse, adding darker woods, handmade ceramics and Japanese restraint. Test: sheepskins and candles point to Scandi; low platform furniture and paper lanterns point to Japandi.

Bohemian vs maximalist

Boho is relaxed and earthy, with layered natural textiles, plants and travel finds. Maximalism is bolder and more curated: saturated colour, pattern on pattern, gallery walls. Test: rattan and jute say boho; velvet and wallpaper say maximalist.

Traditional vs transitional

Traditional keeps ornament, dark woods and formal symmetry. Transitional keeps classic shapes but strips ornament and uses neutral, lighter palettes. Test: damask and mouldings are traditional; a classic sofa in plain greige linen is transitional.

FAQ

What is the most timeless interior style?

Transitional and traditional styles tend to date least because they rely on classic shapes and neutral palettes.

Can I mix Japandi and mid-century modern?

Yes. Both value clean lines and wood. Keep the palette muted and limit mid-century colour to one or two accents.

What's the difference between minimalist and Scandinavian?

Minimalism is about reduction above all; Scandinavian allows more textiles and cosiness while staying simple.
