Dress Codes Decoded: From White Tie to Casual

Every common invitation dress code explained in order of formality, with what to wear and what to avoid.

By the All-Style editors · 6 min read · Updated November 26, 2026

In this guide
  1. The formality ladder
  2. Reading the invitation
  3. The codes people get wrong
  4. Quick fixes when you're unsure

The formality ladder

Dress codes form a ladder. From most to least formal: White Tie → Black Tie → Black Tie Optional → Formal / Cocktail → Cocktail (Semi-formal) → Business Formal → Business Casual → Smart Casual → Casual. When in doubt, aim one rung above what you think and keep accessories simple.

Reading the invitation

Time, venue and wording give clues. Evening events lean more formal than daytime. A hotel ballroom suggests formality; a garden or beach suggests lighter fabrics and shoes that cope with grass or sand. Words like festive, garden party or beach formal modify the base code: same formality, adjusted for setting.

The codes people get wrong

  • Black tie optional: a tuxedo is welcome but a dark, well-tailored suit and tie is acceptable; gowns or elegant cocktail dresses work.
  • Cocktail: not jeans, not floor-length. Think a suit (tie optional at many events) or a knee-to-midi dress.
  • Smart casual: one smart piece plus one casual piece, for example dark jeans with a blazer.
  • Festive attire: cocktail formality plus sparkle, velvet or seasonal colour.

Quick fixes when you're unsure

Ask the host directly; it's normal. Otherwise choose dark colours, good fit and polished shoes, which read correctly across several codes. Our Dress Code Decoder gives a quick outfit formula for each code, and you can get help styling a specific event.

FAQ

Can I wear black to a wedding?

In most Western contexts, yes, especially for evening and formal weddings, but check cultural and family expectations first.

What does 'semi-formal' mean?

It's usually treated as cocktail attire: suits and knee-to-midi dresses.

Is a suit acceptable for black tie?

Only if the invitation says 'black tie optional'. For strict black tie, wear a tuxedo or formal gown.
