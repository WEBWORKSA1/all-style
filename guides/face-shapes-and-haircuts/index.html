Face Shapes and Haircuts: A Practical Matching Guide

How to measure your face shape and which haircuts, fringes and lengths balance each one.

By the All-Style editors · 6 min read · Updated November 26, 2026

In this guide
  1. Measure in two minutes
  2. The six shapes and what balances them
  3. Texture beats shape
  4. Talking to your stylist

Measure in two minutes

Pull hair back and use a soft tape measure or a photo taken straight-on. Measure forehead width (across the widest part), cheekbone width, jaw width (from below one ear to the chin, doubled) and face length (hairline to chin). Note whether your jaw is rounded, square or pointed. The Face Shape Finder does the maths for you.

The six shapes and what balances them

  • Oval: length about 1.5× width, gently rounded jaw. Most cuts work; try a bob or curtain bangs.
  • Round: similar length and width, soft jaw. Add height and length: long layers, side parts, a textured crop with volume on top.
  • Square: strong, angular jaw, similar widths. Soften with layers, waves and curtain bangs; avoid blunt jaw-length cuts.
  • Oblong (rectangle): length clearly longer than width. Add width: fringes, waves, shoulder-length cuts.
  • Heart: wide forehead, narrow pointed chin. Balance with chin-length volume, side-swept fringe or a pixie with soft pieces.
  • Diamond: wide cheekbones, narrow forehead and jaw. Try fringes and chin-length volume, like a wolf cut or shag.

Texture beats shape

Face shape is only half the story. Hair density, texture (straight, wavy, curly, coily), cowlicks and how much time you'll spend styling matter just as much. A cut that suits your face but fights your texture won't survive your morning routine.

Talking to your stylist

Bring two or three reference photos of people with similar hair texture, say how many minutes you'll style it daily, and ask what will happen as it grows out. If you want a stylist who specialises in your hair type, request a match.

FAQ

Can men use the same face-shape rules?

Yes. The same balancing principles apply; men often adjust with top height, fade height and beard shape.

What if I'm between two shapes?

Most people are. Follow the advice for the feature you most want to balance, such as a strong jaw or a long face.

Do glasses follow the same rules?

Broadly, yes: contrast your face shape. Angular frames suit round faces; rounder frames soften square faces.
