Interiors

Biophilic

Nature-led rooms for better wellbeing.

aliverestorativefresh
Get an interior designer for this style

Also called: biophilic design, biophilic interior, jungalow

What is Biophilic?

Biophilic design connects people with nature indoors through plants, natural light, natural materials, water and organic patterns. It is linked with lower stress and better focus.

Origins. Builds on the 'biophilia' hypothesis (humans' innate attraction to nature) popularised in the 1980s, applied to architecture and workplaces from the 2000s.

The palette

Tap a colour to copy its hex code.

Signature elements

  • Abundant plants and living walls
  • Maximised daylight
  • Natural wood and stone
  • Organic, curved shapes
  • Views of nature
  • Water features or nature sounds

Materials & finishes

  • Wood
  • Stone
  • Cork
  • Rattan
  • Living plants

Do

  • Group plants for impact
  • Prioritise light and views
  • Use natural textures you can touch

Don't

  • Use fake plants everywhere
  • Block windows with furniture

Start in three steps

  1. Add three plants of varying heights
  2. Move seating toward a window
  3. Bring in a natural-wood piece

Who it suits & budget tip

Plant lovers and anyone who works from home.

Budget tip: Propagate cuttings to fill a room with plants for nearly nothing.

Frequently asked questions

What is Biophilic?

Biophilic design connects people with nature indoors through plants, natural light, natural materials, water and organic patterns. It is linked with lower stress and better focus.

What colours go with Biophilic?

A typical Biophilic palette uses leaf (#4F7942), fern (#8DB580), bark (#6B4F3A), sky (#BFD7E6) and stone.

How do I start with Biophilic?

1. Add three plants of varying heights. 2. Move seating toward a window. 3. Bring in a natural-wood piece.

What styles go well with Biophilic?

Biophilic blends naturally with Bohemian Interior, Japandi, Modern Organic.

Who suits Biophilic?

Plant lovers and anyone who works from home.
