Interiors

Scandinavian

Light, simple, cosy: designed for long dark winters.

brightcosypractical
Get an interior designer for this style

Also called: scandinavian design, scandi interior, nordic interior, hygge

What is Scandinavian?

Scandinavian interiors use white or pale walls, light wood floors, simple functional furniture and soft textiles to maximise light and comfort. Hygge (cosiness) is the emotional core.

Origins. Developed across Denmark, Sweden, Norway and Finland in the 20th century, with a strong democratic, functional design ethos.

Tap a colour to copy its hex code.

Signature elements

  • Light wood floors
  • Simple functional sofa
  • Sheepskin and wool throws
  • Candles and layered lighting
  • Wishbone-style chairs
  • Indoor plants

Materials & finishes

  • Birch and pine
  • Wool
  • Sheepskin
  • Cotton
  • Ceramic

Do

  • Maximise natural light
  • Layer textures for warmth
  • Choose functional over decorative

Don't

  • Use heavy, dark curtains
  • Over-decorate

Start in three steps

  1. Paint walls a warm white
  2. Add a wool throw and sheepskin
  3. Layer three light sources

Who it suits & budget tip

Families and anyone who wants a bright, livable home.

Budget tip: Flat-pack furniture plus quality textiles gets you 80% of the way.

Frequently asked questions

What is Scandinavian?

Scandinavian interiors use white or pale walls, light wood floors, simple functional furniture and soft textiles to maximise light and comfort. Hygge (cosiness) is the emotional core.

What colours go with Scandinavian?

A typical Scandinavian palette uses snow (#F7F7F4), birch (#E2CFAE), soft grey (#BFC3C2), black accent (#1E1E1E) and dusty blue.

How do I start with Scandinavian?

1. Paint walls a warm white. 2. Add a wool throw and sheepskin. 3. Layer three light sources.

What styles go well with Scandinavian?

Scandinavian blends naturally with Japandi, Minimalist Interior, Mid-Century Modern.

Who suits Scandinavian?

Families and anyone who wants a bright, livable home.
