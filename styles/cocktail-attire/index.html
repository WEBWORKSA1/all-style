Cocktail Attire

Dressy, festive and knee-to-midi length.

festivechicsocial
Get a wedding or event stylist for this style

Also called: cocktail attire, semi-formal, cocktail dress

What is Cocktail Attire?

Cocktail attire sits between black tie and business: suits (ties optional at many events) for men, and knee-to-midi dresses, dressy jumpsuits or elegant separates for women. It allows more colour and personality.

Origins. Grew out of 1920s cocktail parties and became a standard dress code mid-century.

The palette

Tap a colour to copy its hex code.

What it includes

  • Dark suit with or without tie
  • Knee-to-midi dress
  • Dressy jumpsuit
  • Heels or polished shoes
  • Statement jewellery
  • Small clutch

Fabrics & materials

  • Wool suiting
  • Silk
  • Satin
  • Velvet
  • Crepe

Do

  • Use colour and texture
  • Match formality to the venue and time
  • Bring a layer for evening

Don't

  • Wear jeans or sneakers
  • Go floor-length unless the event leans formal

Start in three steps

  1. Pick a dark suit or knee-length dress
  2. Add one statement accessory
  3. Check the venue (garden? ballroom?)

Who it suits & budget tip

Weddings, parties, launches and receptions.

Budget tip: Rent a statement dress or style a suit you already own.

Frequently asked questions

What is Cocktail Attire?

Cocktail attire sits between black tie and business: suits (ties optional at many events) for men, and knee-to-midi dresses, dressy jumpsuits or elegant separates for women. It allows more colour and personality.

What colours go with Cocktail Attire?

A typical Cocktail Attire palette uses jewel blue (#1D4E89), ruby (#9B1B30), black (#151515), champagne (#E6D3B3) and emerald.

How do I start with Cocktail Attire?

1. Pick a dark suit or knee-length dress. 2. Add one statement accessory. 3. Check the venue (garden? ballroom?).

What styles go well with Cocktail Attire?

Cocktail Attire blends naturally with Black Tie, Festive Attire, Soft Glam.

Who suits Cocktail Attire?

Weddings, parties, launches and receptions.
